A Practical Guide to Better Sleep

Better sleep often begins before bedtime. Small changes to timing, light, activity, and your evening routine can make rest easier to reach.

Try these first:
  • Keep wake-up time reasonably consistent.
  • Create a short wind-down routine.
  • Make the bedroom darker, quieter, and comfortable.

Anchor the morning

A steady wake-up time and morning light can support your body’s daily rhythm. Adjust gradually when your schedule allows rather than expecting an immediate transformation.

Build a landing routine

Use the final 20 to 30 minutes of the day to reduce stimulation. Dim lights, prepare for tomorrow, and choose a quiet activity. A short repeated sequence can become a useful signal that the day is ending.

Protect the sleep environment

  • Reduce avoidable light and noise.
  • Keep work and stressful tasks out of bed when possible.
  • Notice whether late caffeine, alcohol, heavy meals, or screens affect you.
  • Get out of bed briefly for a calm activity if you remain wide awake.

Frequently asked questions

How quickly can sleep habits help?

Some changes feel useful immediately, but consistent improvement often takes repeated practice.

Should I force myself to sleep?

Trying harder can increase frustration. Focus instead on creating conditions that allow sleep to occur.
