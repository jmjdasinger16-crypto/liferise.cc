Relationships

How to communicate better

Better communication is less about saying the perfect thing and more about creating enough safety, clarity, and patience for both people to stay in the conversation.

Start here: listen for meaning, speak from your own experience, and solve one issue at a time.

Why conversations break down

Many difficult conversations go wrong because both people are trying to defend themselves, prove a point, or get relief from frustration at the same time. When the nervous system feels threatened, people interrupt, assume motives, raise their voice, or shut down.

The goal is not to remove all emotion. It is to make the conversation steady enough that each person can understand what is actually being said.

A simple communication framework

  1. Pause before reacting. Take one breath and decide what outcome you want from the conversation.
  2. Describe the specific situation. Focus on what happened rather than labeling the other person.
  3. Explain your experience. Use language such as “I felt,” “I noticed,” or “I need.”
  4. Ask for their view. Give the other person room to explain what they intended or experienced.
  5. Agree on one next step. End with a clear, realistic action instead of trying to solve everything at once.

Use fewer accusations

Statements such as “you always” and “you never” often cause the other person to defend themselves. A more useful structure is: “When this happened, I felt this way, and I would like us to try this next time.”

Listen to understand

Listening does not require agreement. It means showing that you understand the other person’s perspective before presenting your own. Try summarizing what you heard and asking, “Did I understand that correctly?”

Choose the right time

Important conversations are harder when someone is exhausted, rushing, intoxicated, or already overwhelmed. Agreeing to return to the issue at a specific time can be more productive than forcing an immediate resolution.

Frequently asked questions

What is the fastest way to improve communication?

Slow down, repeat back what you heard, and ask a clarifying question before giving your response.

How do I communicate without becoming defensive?

Pause, stay focused on the specific behavior or issue, and remind yourself that feedback does not define your entire character.

What if the other person will not communicate calmly?

Set a respectful boundary and suggest returning to the conversation later. You are not required to remain in a conversation that becomes threatening or abusive.
